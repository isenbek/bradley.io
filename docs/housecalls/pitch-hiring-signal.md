# The hiring-signal letter

For posted demand: a company with an open data-engineering or data-platform
role. The posting IS the signal, so this letter never has a dishonest send.
Known live targets from day-one recon: Steelcase (Data Analytics Engineer),
Meijer (Senior Data Engineer), MillerKnoll (Data Scientist, AI & Data Science
team). One email, sent by Brad.

The stance that keeps this letter honest: we are not competing with the hire.
The pitch is the gap between posting and start date, plus the backlog that
created the posting, plus a better first day for whoever they eventually hire.

## The slots

- `{FIRST_NAME}`, `{COMPANY}`: obvious. Prefer the hiring manager over HR.
- `{ROLE_TITLE}`: the posting's own words.
- `{POSTING_DETAIL}`: one specific from the posting proving we read it (a named
  tool, a responsibility line), not a restatement of the title.
- `{PHYSICAL_ADDRESS}`: CAN-SPAM footer.

## Subject lines

1. `While you fill the {ROLE_TITLE} seat`
2. `Your {ROLE_TITLE} posting, read closely`
3. `The backlog behind the job posting`

## The letter

---

{FIRST_NAME},

You are hiring a {ROLE_TITLE}, and {POSTING_DETAIL}. A posting like that
usually means the work is hurting now, and a good hire is months away between
sourcing, notice periods, and ramp-up.

I am a data engineer in Grand Rapids, fifteen years in: a data platform that
integrated 4.9 billion data points from more than 10,000 sources, a messaging
system holding 99.9% uptime across millions of messages. I can start on the
hurting part next week, on a fixed scope you approve in writing first.

To be clear about what I am not: I am not a replacement for your hire, and I
will not try to talk you out of making one. What I offer is the gap. When your
person starts, they inherit working pipelines and written documentation instead
of a backlog and a mystery, which is the difference between a first month and a
first year. If it helps, I am happy to give a technical read on finalists too;
I have sat on that side of the table.

My rates are public at bradley.io/services: $150 to $275 an hour, projects
$25K to $100K. If the honest answer is that you should just wait for your hire,
I will say that in the first meeting and leave you alone.

Full disclosure, because it is the whole point: this letter was drafted by an
AI that I operate, every word was reviewed and signed by me, and the entire
operation is documented in the open at housecalls.bradley.io. We are out to
prove AI can be used for good, at a reasonable price.

Bradley Isenbek
bradley.io · Grand Rapids, Michigan
{PHYSICAL_ADDRESS}

P.S. If this is not relevant, one reply saying so and you will never hear from
us again. That is a promise, and it is logged.
