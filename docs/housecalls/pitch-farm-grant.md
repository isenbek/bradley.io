# The farm grant letter

The farm vertical's first letter. The signal it binds to is public record:
MDARD announced the grant, named the farm, named the dollar figure, and
described the buildout. Congratulating someone on a press release is not
surveillance; it is reading the newspaper. Everything else follows the
house rules: the AI drafted it, Brad signs it, one email forever, the
disclosure is in the body.

## Who gets this letter

A Michigan farm or farm-facing producer with a fresh, dated, linked
grant award (MDARD FAIP, value-added, infrastructure) whose funded work
has a wiring, controls, refrigeration, automation, or data shape. The
letter names their actual project because their actual project was
announced to the state.

## The letter

Subject: The {PROJECT_NOUN} in your MDARD grant, and the part nobody
quotes honestly

{FIRST_NAME},

Congratulations on the {AMOUNT} MDARD award. I read the announcement
({ANNOUNCEMENT_DATE}), and I am writing about one specific part of your
project: {PROJECT_SPECIFIC_SENTENCE, e.g. "an automated filling and
packaging line is two-thirds electrical, controls, and data plumbing,
and that is the part that usually arrives over budget and
under-explained"}.

I run a small tech-service shop out of Grand Rapids. The trades side:
controls, sensors, networks, the wiring between machines and the
software that watches them. The honest side, which is the reason to
read one more paragraph: my prices are posted publicly like a plumber's,
my AI drafts letters like this one and I sign them, and every company I
write to is logged on a public ledger, including the ones that never
answer. You can verify all of it before replying:
housecalls.bradley.io

Two things I believe that most vendors selling to farms do not say out
loud: your equipment data belongs to you, not to a subscription, and
anything we build for you should keep working if I disappear. When we
wire something, you get the documentation, the passwords, and your own
data in your own hands.

If the {PROJECT_NOUN} buildout could use a second set of eyes, an
estimate, or just a sanity check on a vendor quote, reply and I will
come out. If not, no reply needed and none will follow.

Bradley Isenbek
bradley.io · Grand Rapids, Michigan

P.S. Full disclosure: an AI I operate found the MDARD announcement,
drafted this letter, and logged it on the public ledger; I read it,

works, which is the pitch. One email is all we send, ever. A reply of
"no thanks" means you never hear from us again, and we log that too.

## Slots

- `{FIRST_NAME}`: a real person from the farm's own published pages;
  never guessed, never scraped past a wall.
- `{AMOUNT}`: the granted figure, from the release.
- `{ANNOUNCEMENT_DATE}`: the release date; the letter carries its date
  like every honest signal.
- `{PROJECT_NOUN}`: their words for the thing ("packing line", "goat
  dairy", "hoophouses").
- `{PROJECT_SPECIFIC_SENTENCE}`: the one technical observation that
  proves a human-grade read of their actual project. Written fresh per
  letter, never boilerplate.

## Why this letter is honest

- The signal is a state press release: dated, public, linked.
- The letter offers the trades work the grant already funds, not a new
  need invented for them.
- No urgency, no follow-up sequence, no "circling back". One email.
- The data-ownership paragraph is doctrine, not marketing: local-first,
  documented handoff, the customer keeps the keys.
